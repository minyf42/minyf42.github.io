*Independent research · NYU Shanghai, with [Prof. Hua Shen](https://hua-shen.org/) · Mar 2026 – Present*

I set out to adapt LLM explanations for autistic readers through prompting. Instead I found that when a prompt disclosed autism, the model’s concept explanations came out 25–30% shorter (n = 96), yet still passed standard readability metrics. Those metrics reward short words and simple sentences, and ignore how sentences relate to one another.

To test whether the loss happens at the level of discourse, I’m piloting an annotation scheme based on the Penn Discourse Treebank (PDTB-3). Early results show autistic annotators sometimes supply different connectives for the same sentence pairs, which suggests different readings of how the ideas relate. The next stages are a discourse-level accessibility metric, retraining models on comprehension rather than readability, and a comprehension study with autistic adults, for which an IRB protocol is drafted.

Meanwhile, I’m leading a scoping review with Ruoxuan Li of what makes text readable for autistic readers: which metrics are used, what they claim, and what evidence supports them (target journal: Autism).
