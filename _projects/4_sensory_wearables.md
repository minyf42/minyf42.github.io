*Exploration · In progress*

Electrodermal activity, the skin’s electrical response to stress and arousal, could help detect and predict burnout, but only if it’s recorded continuously. For many autistic people, current wearables are hard to keep on: a hard band pressing against the skin and sweat trapped underneath add up to sensory overload, so the device comes off and the data has gaps.

I’m exploring sensing built into clothing that already sits against the skin, such as a lace collar or stockings, so the sensor feels like fabric rather than a device. To prototype, I’m learning 3D printing and Gu embroidery (顾绣), a traditional Shanghai needlework technique, with the aim of stitching electrodes directly into the textile. In the long run, this data could feed the energy model in my capacity-aware scheduler.
