**In progress, with [Ruoxuan Li](https://tomlandhilarious.github.io).**

Large language models are increasingly asked to rewrite text to be “autism-friendly”. How would we know whether a rewrite actually helps? Today the answer usually comes from readability metrics and writing guidelines, and it is unclear whether either was ever checked with autistic readers.

This review is the first step of a larger project on neuro-friendly LLMs. It maps three things for English text:

- the readability metrics proposed or validated for autistic readers
- the text properties that guidelines and autistic people say matter
- the properties that have actually been tested with autistic readers

Where these three line up, existing metrics can guide model rewrites. Where they don’t, the gaps show what a better metric, one grounded in autistic readers’ comprehension, needs to measure. We plan to use that metric to evaluate and train LLM rewriting.

**Progress so far:**

- Searched six databases (about 2,200 records).
- Piloted a screening codebook over three rounds of double coding, with a fourth under way.
- Mapped the prior reviews.
- Used citation chaining to close gaps in the search.

Next: formal screening and coding; we plan to submit the review to *Autism* in late 2026.
